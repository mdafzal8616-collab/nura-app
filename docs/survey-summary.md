# Survey Summary — "Young Muslims: Daily Life & Growth Survey"

Full detail lives in `CLAUDE.md` (Section B9). This file is the quick-reference version.

## Method
- Anonymous Google Form, 24 responses (target 20-30)
- Distributed partly via a poster at a masjid
- **Privacy issue found:** the form was not actually anonymous — 20 of 24 rows carried a real signed-in Gmail address. Fixed by turning off "Collect email addresses" in Form Settings; already-collected email data was deleted from the response sheet. Standing rule going forward: verify anonymity settings before sending any future survey.

## Respondents
- 17 male / 7 female (71% / 29% — skewed male)
- Age: 15 were 18-24, 5 under 18, 4 25-30

## Key findings
- **Hardest part of daily life:** salah/namaz 42%, habits/routine 38%, phone/screen time 38%, confidence/stress 33%, studies/career 33%, islamic learning 29%, sleep 29%
- **After missing a prayer/habit:** restart soon 41%, feel guilty and delay 36%, forget about it for the day 18% — **54% land in guilt or avoidance**
- **What would help daily:** small daily plan 54%, gentle reminder 29%, supportive AI 25%, private reflection 17%
- **What makes people quit an app:** too many ads 46%, too many notifications 21%, hard to use 13%, privacy concerns 13%

## Why Prayer Check-in + Reset Today was picked first
Salah/namaz is the single largest pain point, and the guilt-or-avoidance reaction after missing a prayer (54%) is exactly what "Reset Today" treats. The two most-wanted supports — a small daily plan and a gentle reminder — both fit inside this loop.

**Caveat:** sample skews 71% male — treat this as a strong signal, not proof.
